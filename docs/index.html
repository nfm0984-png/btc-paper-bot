How the bot trades

This is a paper bot for Kalshi’s KXBTC15M market — “BTC price up in the next 15 mins?” It never places a real order. Yes wins if the CF Benchmarks BRTI average in the last 60 seconds of the window is at or above the strike. No wins if it finishes below.

  1. Head-to-head $100 race. Two paper books start at the same moment with $100 each so you can watch which style actually makes more money. Both use $4–$20 per trade ($4 / $6 / $8 by signal strength, then 2× after a win, hard-capped at $20), plus real Kalshi taker fees. Every ticket’s total cost (including fees) stays between $4 and $20.
  2. Version 1 · Active Multi-Trade can take more than one trade in the same 15-minute window — up to 3 tickets, one new entry per 5-minute check, as long as at least 2 minutes remain, the ask is 5¢–95¢, and cash allows. It scores Yes vs No from BTC vs strike, EMA 9/21/50, MACD, RSI, the 5-minute trend, and the ask, then adds to its side on confirmation (or a better price) or flips if momentum turns. Every entry gets a one-sentence reason. All open tickets in that window settle together when Kalshi grades the contract.
  3. Version 2 · Strict Rules follows the learned strategy in strategy_config.json (currently v2). It takes at most one trade per 15-minute window, and only when every trend, strike-gap, and price filter passes. Otherwise it logs WAIT with a one-line reason.
  4. Buy Yes (Version 2) when the 1-minute trend is cleanly bullish (EMA 9 above 21, price above EMA 50, MACD histogram positive, RSI in its bullish band), the 5-minute trend agrees, BTC is far enough from the strike, the Yes ask is inside the learned band, and enough time remains.
  5. Buy No (Version 2) is the bearish mirror.
  6. Wait (Version 2) when those conditions conflict, the EMAs are flat, the window is almost over, or Kalshi’s price is already too expensive.
  7. Sell early if the live bid hits ~80¢ or +15¢ from entry (take profit), drops 12¢ (cut loss), or momentum and BTC-vs-strike both flip against you. Otherwise the bot holds to official settlement.
  8. Sizing. A normal qualifying signal buys a $4 ticket. A strong signal (clearer EMA / MACD / RSI alignment and more distance from the strike) buys $6. A top-conviction signal buys $8.
  9. Double after a win (press). If a trade hits (wins), the next trade is 2× that winning stake, hard-capped at $20 — $4 → $8, $6 → $12, $8 → $16, then $20 max — and never more than the cash left in that book. Every ticket’s total cost, including the Kalshi taker fee, is at least $4 and at most $20. If the pressed trade loses, sizing resets to $4–$8. The $100 race books, the archived 1-hour $40 run, and the ongoing $100 book each track this separately.
  10. Fees. Each race book starts at $100 (earlier $40 race books were lifted to $100 by adding cash, so past trades and P&L stayed the same). The ongoing book starts at $100. The completed 10:59–11:59 AM PT 1-hour $40 run is archived on the Trades tab. Every ticket uses Kalshi’s real taker fee: ceil(0.07 × contracts × price × (1 − price)).
  11. Learns every hour. A GitHub Action pulls newly settled windows, walk-forward tests new parameters on unseen hours (train on the older ~70%, validate on the newest ~30%), and only promotes a change when validation P&L is clearly better — enough trades, a positive unseen profit, a meaningful edge over the live rules, and a 3-hour cooldown so it doesn’t flip-flop on noise. That run is free and does not use Grok Bot.
Paper trading only. Not financial advice. A backtest is not a promise of future results. Kalshi settlement uses CF Benchmarks BRTI, which can differ slightly from Coinbase’s last price.
