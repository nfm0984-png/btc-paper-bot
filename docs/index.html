How the bot trades

This is a paper bot for Kalshi’s KXBTC15M market — “BTC price up in the next 15 mins?” It never places a real order. Yes wins if the CF Benchmarks BRTI average in the last 60 seconds of the window is at or above the strike. No wins if it finishes below.

  1. Buy Yes when the 1-minute trend is cleanly bullish (EMA 9 above 21, price above EMA 50, MACD histogram positive, RSI 52–75), the 5-minute trend agrees, BTC is at least $20 above the strike, the Yes ask is 35¢–72¢, and at least 2 minutes remain.
  2. Buy No is the bearish mirror (EMA 9 below 21, price below EMA 50, MACD negative, RSI 25–48, BTC at least $20 below the strike, No ask 35¢–72¢).
  3. Wait when those conditions conflict, the EMAs are flat, the window has under 2 minutes left, or Kalshi’s price is already too expensive (above 72¢).
  4. Sell early if the live bid hits ~80¢ or +15¢ from entry (take profit), drops 12¢ (cut loss), or momentum and BTC-vs-strike both flip against you. Otherwise the bot holds to official settlement.
  5. Sizing. A normal qualifying signal buys a $2 ticket. A strong signal (clearer EMA / MACD / RSI alignment and more distance from the strike) buys $3. A top-conviction signal buys $4.
  6. Double after a win (press). If a trade hits (wins), the next trade is 2× that winning stake — $2 → $4, $3 → $6, $4 → $8 — capped at $8 and never more than the cash left in that book. If the pressed trade loses, sizing resets to $2–$4. If it wins, the bot keeps pressing 2×, still capped at $8. The 1-hour $40 run and the ongoing $100 book each track this separately.
  7. Fees. The 1-hour run starts at $40. The ongoing book starts at $100. Every ticket uses Kalshi’s real taker fee: ceil(0.07 × contracts × price × (1 − price)).
Paper trading only. Not financial advice. A backtest is not a promise of future results. Kalshi settlement uses CF Benchmarks BRTI, which can differ slightly from Coinbase’s last price.
