How the bot trades Kalshi’s 15-minute BTC contract

This is a paper bot for Kalshi’s KXBTC15M market — “BTC price up in the next 15 mins?” It never places a real order. Yes wins if the CF Benchmarks BRTI average in the last 60 seconds of the window is at or above the strike (the prior window’s close). No wins if it finishes below.

  1. Market. The live 15-minute Kalshi contract, paired with Coinbase 1-minute and 5-minute BTC/USD candles (Kraken fallback).
  2. Buy Yes when the 1-minute trend is cleanly bullish (EMA 9 above 21, price above EMA 50, MACD histogram positive, RSI 52–75), the 5-minute trend agrees, BTC is at least $20 above the strike, the Yes ask is between 35¢ and 72¢, and at least 2 minutes remain.
  3. Buy No is the bearish mirror (EMA 9 below 21, price below EMA 50, MACD negative, RSI 25–48, BTC at least $20 below the strike, No ask 35¢–72¢).
  4. Wait when those conditions conflict, the EMAs are flat, the window has under 2 minutes left, or Kalshi’s price is already too expensive (above 72¢) — there’s no edge in buying a 90¢ Yes.
  5. Sell early if you’re in a trade and the live bid hits ~80¢ or +15¢ from entry (take profit), drops 12¢ (cut loss), or momentum and BTC-vs-strike both flip against you. Otherwise the bot holds to official settlement.
  6. Sizing & fees. $100 starting paper cash, a flat $5 stake per trade, and Kalshi’s real taker fee: ceil(0.07 × contracts × price × (1 − price)), rounded up to the next cent, on every buy and every early sale.
Paper trading only. Not financial advice. A backtest is not a promise of future results. Kalshi settlement uses CF Benchmarks BRTI, which can differ slightly from Coinbase’s last price.
